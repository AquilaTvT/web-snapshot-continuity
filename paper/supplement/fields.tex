\section{What a field can certify}\label{sec:fields}

\subsection{Statement}
Let $G=(V,E)$ be a contract with certifiable states $R$ and representations
$a$, and assume read fidelity on $R$. A \emph{field of the state} is any
function $f$ on $R$; its observation at $q$ is $(b(q),f(q))$, and it is sound if
for all $q,q'\in R$ with equal observations every path from $q$ to $q'$ stays in
$P_{a(q)}$.

\begin{definition}[Finite refinement]\label{def:refinement}
A \emph{finite refinement} of $G$ is a finite contract $G'=(V',E')$ with a
surjective map $\pi:V'\to V$ that sends every transition of $G'$ to a
transition of $G$ and lifts every path: for every $v'\in V'$ and every path of
$G$ that starts at $\pi(v')$, some path of $G'$ starts at $v'$ and maps onto it.
A state $v'$ inherits the representation, body and certifiability of
$\pi(v')$; its fields may also expose the added state.
\end{definition}

A counter, a log of recent operations or any other bounded memory that the
server keeps without forbidding an operation gives a finite refinement. Because
captures are passive, a reader cannot constrain which path the refinement
takes.

\begin{proposition}[What a field can certify]\label{prop:repair}
Assume read fidelity. \textup{(i)} Some field computed from the state is sound
iff every strongly connected component of $G$ that contains a state of $R$ has
one representation. \textup{(ii)} If none is, none is in any finite refinement
of $G$, whereas an unbounded count of representation changes always is.
\textup{(iii)} In a store of immutable versions whose current reads return
$(\beta(v),v)$, equal identities certify continuity for every payload
assignment $\beta$ iff no path from a state whose current identity is $v$ back
to such a state passes another current identity or none; a field recoded
statelessly from the returned fields fails whenever they do.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Proof}
\emph{(i)} Suppose a component $C$ contains $q\in R$ and $z$ with $a(z)\ne a(q)$.
Paths $q\leadsto z$ and $z\leadsto q$ exist, and together they form a closed
walk from $q$ to itself through $z$. Any field of the state takes the same
value at both ends, which are the same state, and the bodies agree, so the two
observations are equal while the walk leaves $a(q)$: no field of the state is
sound. Conversely, suppose every component that contains a certifiable state
has one representation, and let $f(q)$ be the index of $q$'s component. If
$q,q'\in R$ have equal observations they lie in one component $C$, and a path
from $q$ to $q'$ cannot leave $C$: a state $w$ on it is reachable from $q$ and
reaches $q'$, which reaches $q$, so $w\in C$. Every state of the path has
representation $a(q)$, and $f$ is sound. Components take $O(|V|+|E|)$ time;
\code{repair()} in \code{src/history_contracts.py} returns either the component
labels, which the independent checker of Section~\ref{sec:continuity} accepts
as a sound field, or a closed walk through another representation. It makes
one pass over the states to record, per component, a representative and one
state of another representation, and a second pass over the certifiable
states, so it is linear; it is tested on a chain of 20{,}000 states.

\emph{(ii), impossibility.} Let $W$ be the closed walk of (i) from $q$ through
$z$. Choose a lift $q_0$ of $q$ ($\pi$ is surjective). Lifting $W$ from $q_0$
gives a walk of $G'$ that passes a lift of $z$ and ends at a lift $q_1$ of $q$;
lifting $W$ again from $q_1$ gives $q_2$, and so on. As $V'$ is finite,
$q_i=q_j$ for some $i<j$, and the lifted walks from $q_i$ to $q_j$ form a closed
walk of $G'$ through a lift of $z$. The lift $q_i$ is certifiable with
$a(q_i)=a(q)\ne a(z)$, so its component in $G'$ mixes representations, and by
(i), whose proof does not use finiteness, no field of $G'$ is sound.
Conversely, a closed walk of $G'$ maps to a closed walk of $G$, so every
refinement of a contract that satisfies (i) satisfies it too.

\emph{(ii), unbounded repair.} Refine $G$ by a counter $c\in\mathbb{N}$ that
increases at every transition that changes the representation and never
decreases, and let the field be $c$. Equal counts at two reads leave no room
for a change between them, so the field is sound. The refinement is infinite,
as (ii) requires; no bound on $c$ is consistent with permitting every path of
$G$ when $G$ has a closed walk through a change.

\emph{(iii)} If a path from a state with current identity $v$ back to such a
state passes $w\ne v$, assign $\beta(w)\ne\beta(v)$; a state with no current
identity is absent, which differs from every payload. Otherwise the current
identity, and with it the payload, stays $v$ between equal identities. Equal
fields $h$ give equal recodings $g(h)$, so every violation persists after a
stateless recoding; a client that keeps state, reads more often or receives
other trusted information has different inputs and is outside the statement.

The condition of (iii) is not necessary for one fixed payload assignment:
$A/v\to A/w\to A/v$ changes identity without leaving $A$. It is sufficient in
a concrete operation model that many stores approximate: immutable data and
marker records with one time-independent strict total order, a committed set
$L$ that transitions only enlarge, and a current read that selects the maximum
of $L$ (absence for a marker or an empty set). For $L\subseteq L'$,
$\max L\le\max L'$, so a displaced head never returns. The model is not
complete: two paths $a_1\to b_1$ and $b_2\to a_2$ with identities $a$ and $b$
never reactivate an identity, yet a global ranking would need $a<b$ and $b<a$.

\subsection{Unbounded state, horizons and finite identifiers}
Part (ii) leaves two ways out. The first is unbounded state, such as a
per-key publication epoch that every change of the current representation
advances, that nothing decreases, and that every current read returns. The
second is a contract that removes the cycle itself: if the contract bounds the
number of changes, for instance within a certification horizon, a bounded
counter suffices, because that contract no longer permits every path of $G$.
The constructed models of Table~\ref{tab:contracts} show both sides: the
counters modulo three and four admit hidden returns, while
\code{durable_epoch} and \code{unique_boot_nonce} are safe only because their
finite models end after a fixed number of changes.

Production identifiers are finite. The GCS object schema declares
\code{generation} as a 64-bit integer, and MediaWiki revision IDs are
integers of a fixed width~\cite{gcsschema,mediawikirevision}. By the argument
of (ii), a finite identifier can be sound only over a horizon in which it is
not reused; a deployment contract has to state that horizon, including what
happens at exhaustion, reset or restoration. This does not suggest that these
services wrap around in practice; it says what a proof that relies on them
must assume.

\subsection{Relation to shared memory}
In shared memory, change can be detected with bounded objects because readers
take part. Afek et al.\ obtain bounded snapshots by letting each scanner and
updater exchange handshake bits, ``each written by one and read by the
other''~\cite{afek1993}, and the lower bounds of Aghazadeh and Woelfel on
ABA-detecting registers built from bounded objects grow with the number of
processes~\cite{aghazadeh2015}. Sulimany, Cory and Petrank show that
linearizable adaptive snapshots, whose \code{Click} takes a snapshot and whose
\code{Observe} reads a component of it, cannot have invisible reads, that is,
\code{Read} operations that perform no write or read-modify-write, under
obstruction freedom, component encapsulation, update-linearization
independence and an oblivious or $k$-bounded \code{Click}~\cite{sulimany2026}.
Their object and guarantee differ from ours: we analyze a finite transcript of
current reads and ask whether a combination existed in every compatible
history, and our bound counts server states rather than accessed components.
Their result does not imply (ii), nor (ii) theirs. Both concern reads that
leave no trace, in different senses: their reads perform no write or
read-modify-write on shared objects, whereas ours leave the modeled contract
state unchanged, an assumption about the supplied contract rather than the
same property of a deployment. And (ii) is narrower than a general price of
passive reads: it applies when a certifiable state can recur through another
representation, and it excludes only finite additions of state that preserve
every permitted path; a finite design that forbids some paths is outside it. Server-driven consistency~\cite{yin2001} keeps per-client leases and
epoch numbers so that clients detect lost invalidations and revalidate; it
keeps caches current and needs a server that tracks its clients, whereas we
ask what a client can prove from fields a server already exposes.

\subsection{Versioned stores}
The documented S3 operations act on a per-key list of versions and delete
markers~\cite{s3restore}. PUT appends a version, DELETE with a version ID
removes that version, and an unqualified DELETE appends a delete marker, which
DELETE with the marker's ID removes again. After PUT $B$ and the deletion of
$B$'s version, and after inserting and deleting a marker, the list is what it
was before. The modeled state is the key's list and its stored representation
metadata; logs, replication state and billing need not return and are not
part of the observation, and headers generated per request, such as
\code{Date} or a request ID, are not fields of this state. The state $[A]$ thus
lies on closed walks through $[A,B]$ (body $B$) and through $[A,\mathit{marker}]$
(absence), and by (i) no field computed from it --- version ID, ETag,
\code{Last-Modified}, size, checksum, or a recoding of them --- certifies
continuity; by (ii) no bounded addition helps. Two repairs remain: a per-key
publication epoch that these deletions also advance and every current read
returns, or a contract in which versioning stays enabled and no principal can
delete a version or a delete marker, so that the list only grows. The MinIO
profile of Section~\ref{sec:platforms} shows why the second is fragile:
denying version deletion blocked both deletions for a restricted publisher,
but Object Lock alone still allowed $A/\mathit{ID}\to\absent\to A/\mathit{ID}$, and
the owner bypassed a bucket deny; Object Lock does not protect delete
markers~\cite{s3lock}.

GCS generations and Azure Blob version IDs satisfy (iii) within their
documented scopes, since deletion promotes nothing and restoration writes a
new generation or version~\cite{gcsversioning,azureversioning}. MediaWiki
edits and reverts allocate new revision IDs, but revision IDs are preserved
across deletion and undeletion~\cite{mediawikirevision}, so a page restored in
full returns as $A/r\to\absent\to A/r$; the positive scope excludes every
administrative deletion path, not only selective undeletion.

\subsection{Checks}
\code{tests/test_history_contracts.py} and \code{scripts/check_certification.py}
compare \code{repair()} with an exhaustive search on every contract with two
or three states, binary representations and every non-empty certifiable
scope: \sRepairChecks{} contracts, \sRepairSound{} of which admit a sound field.
For each, every labeling of the certifiable states (up to renaming) is audited
with the independent checker; a sound labeling exists exactly when the
component criterion holds, the component labels always pass, and every
returned closed walk is checked edge by edge.

For (ii), \code{refine()} builds the product of a contract with a finite memory
whose next value depends on the transition and the current value; every path
lifts from every memory value, so the product is a finite refinement. The
check draws \sRefinementChecks{} random contracts with two to five states and
memories of one to five values (seed 1103) and confirms that the product
admits a sound field exactly when the contract does; \sRefinementFailing{} of
the contracts admit none. It also refines the three-state S3 deletion model
with epochs counted modulo $k$ and saturating at $k-1$, for $k=1,\dots,16$
(\sEpochChecks{} refinements), none of which admits a sound field, and the unit
tests show that an epoch the contract stops after six changes is sound and
passes the independent checker.
